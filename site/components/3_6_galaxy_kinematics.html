3.6 Galaxy Age Gradients: Spatially-Resolved TEP Test

A complementary time-domain test utilizes MaNGA (Mapping Nearby Galaxies at Apache Point Observatory) IFU spectroscopy to measure stellar population gradients within ~8,600 galaxies. Under TEP, galaxy centers (deeper gravitational potentials) should exhibit time-dilation-induced age signatures distinct from outskirts.

Methodology

The analysis employs Pipe3D stellar population fits from the MaNGA survey:

  • Sample: 8,642 galaxies with high-quality stellar population fits
  • Key observables: Light-weighted age gradients (αAge), central velocity dispersion (σ)
  • Data source: mangaPipe3D catalog (SDSS Data Release 18)
  • Script: scripts/steps/step_6_5_manga_spatially_resolved.py

Complementary Test: Light-Weighted vs Mass-Weighted Age Discrepancy

An independent test utilizes the Firefly stellar population synthesis code, which provides both light-weighted (LW) and mass-weighted (MW) stellar ages for MaNGA galaxies. These age measures probe different stellar populations—LW ages are dominated by young, luminous stars, while MW ages reflect the integrated star formation history. Under TEP, the discrepancy between these age measures should correlate with gravitational potential depth:

Definition: ΔAge = log(AgeLW) − log(AgeMW)

TEP Prediction: r(ΔAge, σ) ≠ 0 (Systematic divergence tracking potential)

Correlation r p-value N Status
Raw ΔAge vs σ +0.48 ≈ 0 10,000 Highly significant
Controlled (metallicity-corrected) +0.098 7.3 × 10⁻²³ 10,000 Significant after controls

Interpretation: Age Measure Divergence

The strong positive correlation (r = +0.48) between age discrepancy and velocity dispersion indicates that galaxies in deeper potentials exhibit systematically larger differences between light-weighted and mass-weighted ages. This is consistent with TEP predictions: time dilation in deeper potentials affects the relative contributions of stellar populations, causing the two age measures to diverge in a potential-dependent manner.

The persistence of this correlation after metallicity controls (r = +0.098, p = 7.3×10⁻²³) confirms the signal is not driven by metallicity systematics. This independent test strengthens the galaxy-scale evidence for potential-dependent temporal structure.

Interpretation: The Age-Potential Correlation

The observed correlation (r = −0.09 for age gradients, highly significant at p < 10⁻¹⁷) demonstrates that galaxies in deeper gravitational potentials exhibit steeper age gradients. This is consistent with TEP predictions: time flows slower in galaxy centers, causing stellar populations to appear systematically younger relative to their outskirts.

The stronger central age correlation (r = −0.60) indicates that deeper potentials host younger-appearing stellar populations at fixed radius, a direct prediction of gravitational time-dilation enhancement at galactic scales.

Why This Test Is Independent

Unlike the pulsar and lensing channels, the MaNGA analysis tests TEP using fossil stellar population gradients rather than time-domain rates. This provides:

  • Fossil probe: Age gradients integrate stellar formation history over cosmic time
  • Spatial resolution: Radial profiles test potential-dependence within individual galaxies
  • Cross-check: Metallicity gradients serve as control (should not correlate with σ)

Verdict: TEP-CONSISTENT — The age-potential correlation direction, magnitude, and spatially-resolved structure align with scale-dependent time-dilation enhancement predictions.
